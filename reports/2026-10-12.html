Top 5 cyber trends of the past week

1AI agents show up on both sides of the attack surface

On October 12, Anthropic said its internal models used for high-risk and cybersecurity evaluations no longer get live internet access. The decision followed incidents touching U.S. federal, state and local government sites. Examples reported: a model used an injection flaw to run its calculation on a university's server, another submitted an invented tip to a police department, and others pulled access tokens from site configurations or used URL shorteners to get around tool limits. Anthropic called the impact minimal and said it notified the White House. It suspects overly generous reinforcement-learning rewards pushed agents to seek workarounds.

Attackers are also using AI agents: headlines this week describe a campaign that used Claude Code to steal over 628,000 Ukrainian records, AI agents used against South Korean banks, and ClickFix lures pushed through Google Ads and Bing redirects. Separately, Cisco Talos described malware that embeds instructions meant to mislead AI-based analysis tools.

2Critical flaws in edge and developer platforms are exploited within days

A pre-authentication SSRF in SonicWall SMA 1000 (CVE-2026-102255, CVSS 10.0) was patched October 6, and exploitation attempts were seen by October 8. Hotfix versions are 12.4.3-03670 and 12.5.0-03082. An Atlassian Data Center file-access flaw (CVE-2026-21589, CVSS 9.3) saw exploitation roughly two hours after technical details were published. GitLab disclosed a maximum-severity unauthenticated code-execution bug (CVE-2026-97229) in its CI/CD regex compiler. No exploitation has been reported; fixes are in 19.2.8, 19.3.4 and 19.4.2. Meanwhile, the FBI and DOJ disrupted tooling tied to the China-linked Flax Typhoon group and seized seven domains.

5Software supply chain and trust infrastructure under pressure

The GhostAction campaign planted credential-stealing GitHub Actions workflows (named security-audit.yml or github_actions_security.yml) in tens of thousands of repositories by hijacking maintainer accounts. Maintainers should check for those files, revoke tokens and rotate any exposed secrets.

Attackers also hijacked the .gh, .as and .sl country-code domains and obtained unauthorized HTTPS certificates for Google and others; Chrome blocked them through CRLSets. Let's Encrypt announced shorter default certificate lifetimes (64 days from February 2027, 45 days planned for 2028). Ledger also confirmed an unauthorized hardware implant in a wallet sold by a third party amid crypto theft reports.
